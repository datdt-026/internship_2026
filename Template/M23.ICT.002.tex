\begin{abstract}
Vaccine hesitancy remains a public-health concern in the post-COVID-19 period, amplified by misinformation that mixes text and images on social platforms and news sites.
This thesis builds a content-level multimodal Transformer pipeline for COVID-19 / vaccine misinformation detection and for analysing the narratives that characterise post-pandemic hesitancy discourse.
On the CONSTRAINT English fake-news benchmark, a RoBERTa text classifier reaches macro-F1~$0.9719$ and accuracy~$0.9720$.
On the MMCoVaR vaccine-focused news subset with downloaded images, an ablation of text-only, image-only (CLIP), and late-fusion models shows that text dominates (macro-F1~$\approx 0.963$), image-only performance is weak (macro-F1~$\approx 0.554$), and late fusion approximately matches text without clear gains.
BERTopic applied to $5{,}752$ misinformation documents yields a taxonomy of dominant themes, including sensational case counts, politicised framing, contested measures, origin conspiracies, miracle-cure claims, and elite distrust.
The work delivers a reproducible pipeline, empirical evidence on modality contribution, and a communication-oriented narrative map, while scoping TikTok audio and live platform crawling as future work.
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

This chapter introduces the research project, providing the necessary background and motivation for the work.

\section{Context}
\label{sec:context}

The COVID-19 pandemic was accompanied by an ``infodemic'': a rapid surge of true, false, and misleading health information online~\cite{who2020infodemic}.
Even after the acute pandemic phase, vaccine hesitancy---delay in acceptance or refusal of vaccination despite availability~\cite{macdonald2015hesitancy}---continues to affect public-health programmes.
Social platforms and digital news accelerate claims about vaccine safety, efficacy, mandates, and conspiratorial explanations, often faster than fact-checkers can respond.

Contemporary content is multimodal.
A caption may look cautious while an image suggests danger; a long news body may carry the factual claim while a thumbnail frames emotion.
Unimodal text classifiers can miss visual cues; image-only models may ignore the linguistic claim that carries the misinformation.
This thesis therefore studies a multimodal Transformer-based approach that considers text and images for COVID-19 / vaccine misinformation detection, and complements detection with narrative analysis of dominant hesitancy-related themes.

We focus on \emph{content-level} classification: given text and an optional image, predict whether the item is credible or misinformation.
This is complementary to graph-based diffusion modelling and is aligned with practical screening by analysts.

\section{Contribution}
\label{sec:contribution}

The main contributions of this work are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A reproducible multimodal research pipeline (data preparation, training, evaluation, topic analysis) for COVID/vaccine misinformation experiments.
  \item A strong text-only baseline on CONSTRAINT using RoBERTa (macro-F1~$0.9719$).
  \item An ablation study on MMCoVaR comparing text-only, image-only (CLIP), and late fusion, quantifying modality contribution.
  \item A BERTopic-based taxonomy of post-pandemic misinformation narratives for communication-oriented interpretation.
\end{enumerate}

Relative to the original proposal that targeted live X and TikTok streams with audio (Whisper), the internship timeline and API constraints led to a survival scope that preserves the scientific core---text benchmark, multimodal ablation, and narrative analysis---while deferring platform crawling and audio to future work.

\section{Plan}
\label{sec:plan}

\chapter{Related Works}
\label{chap:related-works}

This chapter reviews existing literature related to vaccine hesitancy, COVID-19 misinformation datasets, multimodal detection, and the modelling tools used in this thesis.

\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:literature-review}

\subsection{Vaccine hesitancy and online misinformation}

Vaccine hesitancy is recognised by WHO as a major barrier to immunisation programmes~\cite{macdonald2015hesitancy}.
It is not a simple pro/anti dichotomy: individuals may accept some vaccines while delaying or refusing others, and attitudes shift with trust in institutions, risk perception, and peer influence.
During COVID-19, false claims about ingredients, infertility, microchips, and exaggerated adverse events circulated widely.
The WHO joint statement on managing the COVID-19 infodemic emphasises that misinformation can harm health behaviours as much as the virus itself in some settings~\cite{who2020infodemic}.

Shu et~al.~\cite{shu2017fake} survey fake-news detection on social media and distinguish content-based, social-context, and hybrid approaches.
Content-based methods inspect linguistic style, claim structure, and attached media.
Social-context methods exploit user graphs, retweet cascades, and source credibility over time.
This thesis follows the content-based line: classify the message itself before modelling diffusion graphs.
The choice matches internship constraints and yields models that can screen an individual post or article without requiring a full interaction graph.

Healthcare-specific resources such as CoAID~\cite{cui2020coaid} and ANTi-Vax~\cite{hayawi2022antivax} provide labelled COVID/vaccine claims.
ANTi-Vax releases tweet IDs for labelled vaccine misinformation; however, ID-only releases require hydration under platform API policies and become brittle when tweets are deleted.
We therefore prioritise datasets that ship full text (and, when possible, images) for reproducibility within the internship timeline.
This decision trades some social-media ecological validity for a complete, auditable experimental stack.

\subsection{COVID-19 fake news benchmarks}

Patwa et~al.~\cite{patwa2021fighting} released the CONSTRAINT shared-task dataset of English COVID-related social posts labelled real/fake.
It has become a standard text classification benchmark with strong Transformer baselines.
We use a public Hugging Face mirror of this dataset and map labels to a canonical schema (credible/misinfo).

\subsection{Multimodal misinformation detection}

Multimodal fake-news datasets such as Fakeddit~\cite{nakamura2020fakeddit} demonstrate that combining text and images can help when visual and linguistic signals interact.
For COVID specifically, MM-COVID~\cite{li2020mmcovid} provides multilingual content with linked media, while MMCoVaR~\cite{chen2021mmcovar} focuses on COVID-19 vaccine news and tweets with textual, visual, and temporal information.
We adopt MMCoVaR news articles because they include image URLs and publisher-based reliability labels suitable for multimodal experiments without tweet hydration.

Architecturally, late fusion concatenates modality embeddings before classification, whereas early or cross-attention fusion mixes tokens across modalities.
Late fusion is simpler, more robust to missing modalities, and appropriate under time constraints; cross-attention remains future work.

\subsection{Transformer encoders for text and vision}

Transformers~\cite{vaswani2017attention} underpin modern NLP.
BERT~\cite{devlin2019bert} and RoBERTa~\cite{liu2019roberta} provide contextual text representations widely used for classification.
For images, CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} learns aligned vision--language embeddings from large-scale contrastive pretraining; its vision tower yields transferable image features for downstream tasks, often with a frozen backbone.

\subsection{Topic modelling for narrative analysis}

Beyond accuracy metrics, public-health stakeholders need interpretable themes.
BERTopic~\cite{grootendorst2022bertopic} clusters document embeddings (for example Sentence-BERT~\cite{reimers2019sentence}) and extracts class-based TF--IDF topic words.
We use BERTopic to build a taxonomy of misinformation narratives after detection experiments.

\section{Comparison with Existing Approaches}
\label{sec:comparison}

Prior work often reports high accuracy on COVID text benchmarks, but less frequently reports controlled ablations that separate text and image contribution on the same vaccine-oriented multimodal split.
Publisher-reliability labels, as in MMCoVaR news, are practical yet coarser than claim-level fact-checks; our experiments make this trade-off explicit.
ID-only social datasets are difficult to reproduce under current API constraints; our choice of CONSTRAINT and MMCoVaR privileges end-to-end reproducibility.

Relative to complex cross-attention multimodal architectures, we deliberately evaluate a transparent late-fusion baseline with a frozen CLIP tower.
This design answers a precise question: does adding images improve over a strong RoBERTa text model on this news subset?
As shown later, the answer is essentially no under our setup---a negative but useful result that prevents overclaiming multimodality gains.

Finally, detection alone does not explain discourse structure.
Coupling a classifier with BERTopic narrative analysis addresses the communication need for a map of themes linked to hesitancy, which many detection papers leave as future qualitative work.

\chapter{Contributions}
\label{chap:contribution}

This chapter presents the methods implemented in this thesis: data preparation, encoders, fusion, training, and narrative analysis.
All previous literature context is assumed from Chapter~\ref{chap:related-works}.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Pipeline overview}

The research pipeline has five stages:
(1)~dataset preparation and label normalisation;
(2)~modality-specific encoders;
(3)~classification heads for text, image, and late fusion;
(4)~evaluation with accuracy, precision, recall, and macro-F1;
(5)~BERTopic narrative analysis on misinformation texts.

Formally, let $x = (t, v)$ where $t$ is text and $v$ is an optional image.
We learn $f_\theta(x) \rightarrow y \in \{0,1\}$ with $0=\texttt{credible}$ and $1=\texttt{misinfo}$.

\subsection{Label schema}

All experiments use a binary schema:
\texttt{credible} maps to class~$0$ and \texttt{misinfo} maps to class~$1$.
CONSTRAINT labels \texttt{real}/\texttt{fake} map to \texttt{credible}/\texttt{misinfo}.
MMCoVaR \texttt{reliability}$=1$ (reliable) maps to \texttt{credible}; \texttt{reliability}$=0$ maps to \texttt{misinfo}.

\subsection{CONSTRAINT dataset (text track)}

We use the public Hugging Face distribution of the CONSTRAINT English dataset~\cite{patwa2021fighting}.
After cleaning (URL stripping, whitespace normalisation) we obtain $10{,}700$ samples with official splits (Table~\ref{tab:constraint-stats}).

\subsection{MMCoVaR news dataset (multimodal track)}

MMCoVaR provides vaccine-focused news with image URLs~\cite{chen2021mmcovar}.
We download images from the \texttt{image} column and retain articles with a successfully retrieved local image.
Text is formed by concatenating \texttt{title} and \texttt{body\_text}.
Stratified splits (70/15/15, seed~$42$) yield the statistics in Table~\ref{tab:mmcovar-stats}.
Of $2{,}592$ candidate URLs, $2{,}131$ images were successfully downloaded ($461$ failures due to dead links or timeouts).

News bodies are long (often thousands of characters).
This text richness is central to interpreting later ablation results: linguistic content can already encode publisher-style reliability cues.

\subsection{Text encoder: RoBERTa}

We fine-tune RoBERTa-base~\cite{liu2019roberta} for binary classification.
Input text is tokenised with truncation/padding to a fixed maximum length.
The pooled representation (CLS) is passed to a linear classification head.
The training loss is standard cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = -\sum_{c=0}^{1} \mathbb{1}[y=c]\log p_\theta(c\mid t).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Image encoder: CLIP}

For images we use CLIP ViT-B/32~\cite{radford2021clip} with a frozen vision backbone.
An image $v$ is resized and normalised according to CLIP preprocessing, then encoded to a fixed-dimensional embedding $h_v$.
A linear head maps $h_v$ to class logits for the image-only model.
Frozen features reduce trainable parameters and training time; they also test whether off-the-shelf visual representations already carry veracity signal.

Corrupt or truncated downloads are handled defensively: failed loads produce a zero embedding so that training can continue, at the cost of noise in the image stream.

\subsection{Late fusion}

Late fusion concatenates text and image embeddings:
\begin{equation}
h = [h_t; h_v], \qquad
\hat{y} = \mathrm{MLP}(h),
\end{equation}
where $h_t$ is the RoBERTa CLS vector and $h_v$ is the CLIP image embedding.
The MLP is a small fully connected classifier trained end-to-end with respect to the fusion head (and RoBERTa when unfrozen according to configuration), while CLIP remains frozen in our experiments.

This design is intentionally simple.
If fusion fails to beat text-only, the cause is unlikely to be an overly exotic architecture; rather, the visual modality may not add complementary information under the available labels and image quality.

\subsection{Training protocol}

Training uses AdamW with a moderate learning rate, early stopping on validation macro-F1, and a fixed random seed where applicable.
Checkpoints store the best validation model; final metrics are reported on the held-out test split.
Implementation relies on PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers; configuration files separate CONSTRAINT and MMCoVaR paths so that ablation runs share the same multimodal splits.

Typical hyperparameters used in the delivered codebase include a batch size compatible with GPU memory, a maximum text length suitable for news bodies (with truncation), and a small number of fine-tuning epochs with patience-based early stopping.
Macro-F1 is preferred over accuracy alone because MMCoVaR is class-imbalanced (more credible than misinfo articles in our downloaded subset).

Reproducibility artefacts include JSONL splits, YAML configs, saved checkpoints, and JSON metric files under a project \texttt{results/} directory.
Shell scripts orchestrate the three-way ablation so that text, image, and fusion runs differ only by the modality flag.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

For a binary prediction $\hat{y}$ against gold $y$, we report accuracy and the macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1:
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Precision}_c &= \frac{\mathrm{TP}_c}{\mathrm{TP}_c+\mathrm{FP}_c}, \quad
\mathrm{Recall}_c = \frac{\mathrm{TP}_c}{\mathrm{TP}_c+\mathrm{FN}_c}, \\
\mathrm{F1}_c &= \frac{2\,\mathrm{Precision}_c\,\mathrm{Recall}_c}{\mathrm{Precision}_c+\mathrm{Recall}_c}, \quad
\mathrm{macro\text{-}F1} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}} \mathrm{F1}_c.
\end{align}
Macro-F1 treats both classes equally and is therefore a fairer summary when class frequencies differ.

\subsection{BERTopic narrative pipeline}

After detection experiments, we aggregate documents labelled as misinformation from CONSTRAINT and MMCoVaR ($N=5{,}752$ after filtering).
Long news bodies are truncated (for example to $800$ characters) for embedding efficiency.
Documents are embedded with Sentence-Transformers \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}~\cite{reimers2019sentence} and clustered with BERTopic~\cite{grootendorst2022bertopic} (\texttt{min\_topic\_size}$=15$).
Topic keywords are inspected qualitatively and renamed into human-readable themes for the taxonomy reported in Chapter~\ref{chap:experiments}.

\section{Theoretical Results}
\label{sec:theoretical-results}

This thesis is primarily empirical; we do not claim new learning-theoretic bounds.
Nevertheless, several modelling observations guide interpretation.

First, under a missing-or-weak modality regime, late fusion behaves approximately like a text classifier if $\|h_v\|$ is uninformative or noisy.
In the extreme, if $h_v$ is near zero (failed image loads) or poorly correlated with $y$, the fusion MLP can learn to ignore the image block, recovering text-only behaviour.
This explains why fusion may match text without improving it.

Second, publisher-level reliability is a surrogate label.
Let $y$ denote publisher reliability and $z$ denote claim-level truth.
If images correlate more with layout style than with $z$, while text correlates with both $y$ and $z$, then $\mathrm{I}(y;h_t) \gg \mathrm{I}(y;h_v)$ is expected, and image-only classifiers remain weak.
Our ablation is therefore a test of mutual information between modalities and the available label, not a universal verdict against multimodality.

Third, topic modelling is unsupervised clustering in embedding space.
Themes are interpretable summaries, not causal explanations of hesitancy.
They should be treated as decision-support for communication analysis, validated by human reading of representative documents.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Hardware and software}

Experiments run in a Python virtual environment with PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers/Datasets, CLIP, and BERTopic.
Training uses GPU acceleration when available; evaluation scripts reload the best checkpoint and compute metrics on JSONL splits.

\subsection{Experiment A: CONSTRAINT text baseline}

We train the RoBERTa text classifier on CONSTRAINT train/val splits and evaluate on test.
Early stopping selects the checkpoint with best validation macro-F1.
Metrics reported: accuracy, precision, recall, and macro-F1.

As a classical reference, we also train TF--IDF (1--2 grams, $50{,}000$ features) with class-balanced logistic regression on the same splits, without neural fine-tuning.

\subsection{Experiment B: MMCoVaR multimodal ablation}

On the multimodal MMCoVaR subset, we train three models under identical splits:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Text-only RoBERTa
  \item Image-only CLIP (frozen encoder + linear head)
  \item Late fusion (RoBERTa CLS concatenated with CLIP image embedding + MLP)
\end{enumerate}
All three share the same train/val/test partition so differences reflect modality, not split noise.

\subsection{Experiment C: BERTopic taxonomy}

We run BERTopic on the aggregated misinformation corpus and inspect the largest coherent topics, renaming them for thesis presentation.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Text dominates on MMCoVaR}

News title and body carry most of the reliability signal under publisher labels.
Images alone are weak: thumbnails and illustrative photos often do not encode factual veracity.
Fusion approximately recovers text performance, consistent with the modelling observation in Chapter~\ref{chap:contribution} that uninformative visual features can be ignored by the fusion head.

These findings answer the main empirical questions.
Late fusion does not clearly outperform text-only on this MMCoVaR news subset.
In ablation, text contributes substantially more than image.

\subsection{Why fusion did not help}

Several factors likely contribute.
News bodies are long and informationally dense; images are often decorative.
Publisher reliability is a coarse proxy; visual style may correlate weakly with publisher class.
Late fusion with a frozen CLIP tower may under-adapt visual features to the veracity task.
Missing or truncated images inject noise into the image stream ($\approx 18\%$ URL failures).

The result does not invalidate multimodality in general.
It cautions against assuming that adding CLIP features automatically improves COVID/vaccine news classification when text is already strong.

\subsection{Narratives and hesitancy}

Although not every theme is narrowly about vaccine injection side effects, the taxonomy reveals a multi-narrative ecosystem that can fuel hesitancy indirectly.
Distrust of institutions and elites undermines confidence in recommendations.
Miracle-cure narratives reduce perceived need for vaccination.
Sensational harm and hospital imagery amplify fear responses.
Child and school themes specifically target parental decision-making.

Operationally, the classifier can flag misinformation content at scale; BERTopic then summarises what is being flagged.
Together they form a monitoring loop useful for public-health analysts, even when multimodal fusion does not beat text on every dataset.

\subsection{Qualitative error patterns}

We analysed all RoBERTa mistakes on the CONSTRAINT test set ($60$ errors out of $2{,}140$ samples: $38$ false positives, $22$ false negatives).
Heuristic categories (Table~\ref{tab:error-cats}) show that residual failures are concentrated in short or stylistically ambiguous posts and in hedged claims that mimic news tone.

False positives often involve alarming yet ultimately credible reporting (for example hospitalisation disparities or outbreak updates written in emphatic style).

On MMCoVaR, residual multimodal errors additionally include rare visual--text mismatches, but bodies are text-heavy, so pure image deception is uncommon.

\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

This thesis addressed content-level detection of COVID-19 / vaccine misinformation and the analysis of post-pandemic hesitancy narratives.
A RoBERTa classifier on CONSTRAINT achieved macro-F1~$0.9719$.
On MMCoVaR, ablation showed text dominance (macro-F1~$\approx 0.963$), weak image-only performance, and late fusion approximately matching text.
BERTopic on $5{,}752$ misinformation documents produced a multi-narrative taxonomy spanning sensational statistics, politicisation, contested measures, origin conspiracies, miracle cures, and elite distrust.
The accompanying codebase makes the experiments reproducible.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The original proposal mentioned X and TikTok; experiments use CONSTRAINT social text and MMCoVaR news due to API and time constraints.
Whisper-based TikTok audio analysis was not implemented.
Data are English-centric; transfer to Vietnamese or multilingual settings is untested.
About $18\%$ of MMCoVaR image URLs failed to download.
Publisher reliability is not identical to claim-level fact-checking.
BERTopic solutions vary with seeds and parameters and require human validation.
Ethically, detectors can support public-health response but also risk over-blocking legitimate debate or satire; outputs should be treated as decision support with human review.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

Several directions follow directly from the limitations above.

First, true short-video multimodality requires TikTok (or similar) frames together with Whisper transcripts, so that audio claims and on-screen text can be fused with visuals.
Second, architectural upgrades---cross-attention fusion and unfrozen vision towers---may unlock image signal when labels are claim-level rather than publisher-level.
Third, expanding fact-checked claim labels would tighten the link between model decisions and verifiable truth.
Fourth, a multilingual evaluation set including Vietnamese would increase local relevance for USTH and regional public-health partners.
Fifth, an interactive dashboard linking classifier scores to live narrative trends would turn the research pipeline into an operational monitoring tool.

These extensions preserve the core finding of this thesis: measure modality contribution carefully, keep text baselines strong, and couple detection with narrative analysis rather than accuracy alone.
